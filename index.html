The context

A flat I nearly let go

I bought a 2 BHK flat in Aparna Zenon, Hyderabad. To be honest, my first plan was to sell it. Somewhere along the way I changed my mind and decided to keep it.

The flat made that decision easy. It is above the 20th floor and east facing, so mornings start with sunlight. It has an excellent view, good air flow, a balcony I actually want to sit on, and natural light all through the day. Once I stood in it, I knew I wanted to live there myself.

The panic

Six things everyone told me to expect

Before choosing anyone, I did my research. I spoke to people and read other owners' stories. The same six complaints kept coming up.

1

Interior firms are excellent at sales. They promise ten things and show beautiful 3D designs, then under-deliver most of the time.

2

They say 1–2 months. It takes 3–4 months.

3

Getting a near-perfect result is rare. There is almost always a mismatch in colour, quality or design.

4

They collect 50–80% of the money upfront, and then the delays begin.

5

The owner has to do endless follow-ups. It becomes a constant headache.

6

Poor execution and skill gaps on site are now considered normal.

After hearing all this, I honestly gave up. I assumed every interior firm would be like this, and I started to panic.

My suggestions

If you are about to start your interiors

One good experience does not mean every firm is good, or that every firm is bad. Here is what I would do again, and what I would tell a friend.

  1. Trust finished homes more than 3D renders

    Ask to see a completed flat, ideally in your own community, and speak to that owner directly. Renders show what a firm can draw. A finished home shows what it can build.

  2. Ask past clients how long it actually took

    Ask about the real start and handover dates, not the quoted ones. Then get your own timeline in writing, with milestones.

  3. Link payments to milestones

    Avoid paying most of the amount before work begins. Agree in writing on what gets paid at each visible stage.

  4. Freeze materials and finishes on paper

    Write down laminate and colour codes, board type, hardware brands and lighting in the quotation. See physical samples before you sign. This is how you avoid the colour and quality mismatch.

  5. Have one point of contact

    A single coordinator who answers for the whole project saves you most of the follow-ups.

  6. Visit the site regularly

    Small fixes are cheap when they are caught early. Keep your final payment until a snag list has been walked through and closed.

  7. Get the warranty and after-sales terms in writing

    Know who to call when a hinge or a drawer channel needs attention after handover.
