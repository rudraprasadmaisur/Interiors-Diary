The context

The flat I almost sold

The flat is above the 20th floor and faces east, so the day begins with sunlight. The view is excellent, the air moves freely, the balcony is one you actually want to sit on, and the light is beautiful. A home like this is hard to give up.

The panic

Six warnings I heard from everyone

Before choosing anyone, I did my homework. I asked around and read other owners' stories. The same six warnings came up again and again.

  1. 1

    Sold well, delivered poorly

    Firms are brilliant at sales. They promise ten things and show beautiful designs, then under-deliver most of the time.

  2. 2

    Two months becomes four

    They quote one to two months. It takes three to four.

  3. 3

    Almost right, never right

    There is nearly always a mismatch somewhere, in colour, quality or design.

  4. 4

    Paid upfront, then delayed

    They collect 50–80% of the money first. Then the delays begin.

  5. 5

    You become the project manager

    The owner ends up chasing, following up and firefighting, week after week.

  6. 6

    Execution is a lottery

    Poor workmanship and skill gaps on site are now treated as normal.

By the end of my research I had honestly given up. I assumed every interior firm would be like this.

My suggestions

My advice to a friend, before they sign

One good experience doesn't mean every firm is great, and it doesn't mean the warnings are wrong. It does show that the outcome depends a lot on who you choose and how you set things up. This is what I would do again.

  1. Judge finished homes, not 3D renders

    Renders show what a firm can draw. A finished home shows what it can build. Visit one, ideally in your own community, and talk to the owner.

  2. Ask past clients for real dates

    Ask when work actually started and when it was handed over. Then put your own timeline in writing, with dated milestones.

  3. Pay against progress

    Avoid paying most of the amount before work begins. Agree in writing on what gets paid at each visible stage.

  4. Put every material on paper

    List laminate and colour codes, board type, hardware brands and lights in the quotation, and see physical samples before you sign. This is how you avoid the "almost right" finish.

  5. Insist on one point of contact

    One coordinator who answers for the whole project saves you most of the follow-ups.

  6. Visit the site regularly

    Small fixes are cheap when they are caught early.

  7. Hold the last payment for the snag list

    Walk through the finished flat together, list every snag, and release the final amount once they are fixed. Get warranty and after-sales terms in writing too.
